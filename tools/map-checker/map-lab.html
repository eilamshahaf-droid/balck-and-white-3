Street pattern

Roads

Cross-sections follow the NACTO Urban Street Design Guide (lanes 3.0 to 3.3 m, parking 2.4 m, bike lanes 1.8 m) and New York's 18 m streets and 30 m avenues.

Junctions follow the two biggest roads that meet (Sustainable Safety): boulevards get signals, side streets turn right in and out of them only, avenues meet at signals or roundabouts (FHWA sizes), side streets at stop or give-way signs. Busy side streets become avenues by how many trips pass along them. The expressway meets no street; one tight-diamond interchange where there is room.

Land use weights

Heights

The density layer peaks downtown and at the busier district centres, with bumps where boulevards cross, and falls off unevenly. Taller buildings follow it; houses keep their size. Height range spreads neighbours apart and lets a few landmarks stand out.

Paths and trees

On foot: park paths join the park's entrances through a small square, as people walk them (desire lines), the main path 3 m wide and the rest 2 m; planting kept 2 m off paths. Blocks over about 200 m long get a passage through the middle (walkable-city codes ask for one every 100 to 120 m). Street trees stand every 15 m in the kerbside strip wherever the sidewalk leaves a 1.8 m clear path (San Francisco and Minneapolis sidewalk-zone guides). No road runs inside a park.
